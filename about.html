OC Scanner started with a scanner, a notebook, and a simple frustration: by the time most local stories reached the public, they were hours old and missing context. I set out to fix that — monitoring public safety, national security, intel, and geopolitical developments across Orange County, Southern California, and beyond, and getting verified information out while it still matters.

As an Army veteran, I take sourcing seriously. Everything here runs on open-source methods: corroborate first, label what's still developing, and correct in the open. No affiliation, no agenda — just a clear signal in a noisy feed, available to anyone who wants to know what's happening around them.

— OC Scanner

Editorial standards

Effective November 21, 2026 · These apply to everything we publish: the website, the ticker, the newsletter, and our posts on X, Facebook and Instagram.

Verification

  • Original claims need confirmation. Before we report something as fact, we confirm it with an official source (an agency statement, the National Weather Service, court or public records) or with at least two independent sources that didn't get it from each other.
  • Scanner traffic is a lead, not a fact. First radio reports are often wrong. When we share what we hear before it's confirmed, we say so plainly: "Scanner traffic, unconfirmed."
  • Official information stays official. We don't change the facts, times or areas in warnings and agency statements. We may shorten them and explain them in plain words.
  • A person checks every story. Nothing original about public safety is published without a person reviewing it first.

Labels you'll see

  • Breaking — new, important and confirmed enough to publish now.
  • Developing — the story is still changing; expect updates.
  • Unconfirmed — reported or heard, but not yet verified.
  • Update — new information added to an earlier story, with the time.
  • Correction or Clarification — see corrections below.
  • Analysis or Opinion — our interpretation, kept separate from straight news.
  • Sponsored — paid content. It is always labeled and never written to look like news.

Sourcing and attribution

  • We say where information comes from: the agency, the document, the data feed, or "a witness who sent us video."
  • When another outlet breaks a story first, we credit and link to them.
  • Photos and video carry a credit line. We use someone else's footage only with their permission or when the source allows it, and we credit tipsters only if they ask to be named.
  • Maps, radar and data images name their source (for example, "Radar: NOAA/NWS via Iowa Environmental Mesonet").
  • We protect people who give us information. We don't name a source who asked not to be named.

What we don't publish

Public safety reporting means hearing things that shouldn't go any further. We don't publish:

  • The names of people killed or injured before officials release them or their families have been told.
  • The names or faces of minors involved in crimes or emergencies, unless officials release them for a reason such as a missing child alert.
  • The names of people who haven't been arrested or charged. A person who is arrested is "arrested," not guilty; we say what they are accused of and who is accusing them.
  • Home addresses, license plates, phone numbers, or medical details of private people.
  • Officer or firefighter positions and tactics during an active operation, such as a standoff or a search, where it could put people in danger.
  • Graphic images of victims, or details of how someone took their own life.
  • Anything from encrypted or restricted radio channels.

Photos, video and AI images

  • We may crop, resize and adjust brightness. We don't add, remove or move anything in a news photo or video.
  • We never present an AI-generated or edited image as real. Illustrations and graphics are labeled as such.
  • Hidden photo details such as GPS location are removed when photos are uploaded to our newsroom.

How we use AI

  • We use AI tools, including Anthropic's Claude, to help with routine work: summarizing public headlines for the home page digest, sorting incoming news, suggesting drafts and headlines, and writing captions for weather graphics from official National Weather Service text.
  • AI never decides what is true. A person checks the facts, sources and wording of every story before it's published.
  • AI is not used to invent quotes, sources, photos or events.
  • Anything published with little or no human writing, such as the automated digest, is labeled as automated.

Independence

  • OC Scanner is independent and isn't affiliated with any law enforcement, fire or government agency, political party or campaign.
  • Donors, sponsors and advertisers don't get a say in what we cover or how. Sponsorships are disclosed.
  • Staff and contributors tell us about any personal or financial connection to a story, and step away from covering it when there is one.
  • We cover elections and politics on the facts. We don't endorse candidates or ballot measures.

We're not an emergency alert service

We report fast, but we can miss things and our feeds can go down. For warnings and evacuation orders, sign up for AlertOC and follow the National Weather Service and your local agencies. In an emergency, call 911.

Corrections policy

We make mistakes, and when we do, we fix them in the open. We don't quietly change facts in a story.

What we do

  • Correction — when we got a fact wrong (a name, a number, a place, a time, what happened). We fix the story and add a note at the end that says what was wrong and what is right, with the date. If the error was serious, the note also goes at the top.
  • Clarification — when a story was accurate but unclear or could mislead. We rewrite the part and add a note.
  • Update — when a developing story gets new information. We add it with the time. An update isn't a correction, unless the earlier version was wrong.
  • Small fixes — spelling, grammar and broken links are fixed without a note, as long as the meaning doesn't change.

A correction note looks like this:

Correction (Nov 21, 2026): An earlier version of this story said the fire was in Lake Forest. It was in Mission Viejo.

On social media and the ticker

  • If a mistake went out on X, Facebook or Instagram, we post the correction as a reply or follow-up to the original post so people who saw it can see the fix.
  • We delete a post only when leaving it up could cause harm, such as wrong evacuation information or a wrongly named person. When we do, we post a note saying what we removed and why.
  • Ticker items with an error are corrected or taken down at once.

Removing a story

We rarely unpublish. We'll do it when a story is seriously wrong and can't be fixed, or to protect someone's safety or privacy. When we remove a story, we leave a short note in its place explaining why.

How fast

Errors about public safety, such as the wrong evacuation area, a wrong name or wrong warning times, are fixed as soon as we confirm them. Other reports are reviewed within a day or two.

Report an error

Email news@ocscanner.media with "Correction request" in the subject. Include the story link, what you think is wrong, and how you know. Every story also has a Request a correction link at the bottom. We'll tell you what we decided.

Important

OC Scanner is an independent news and monitoring account. We are not affiliated with any law enforcement, fire, or government agency. Information is shared for situational awareness and may be preliminary or unconfirmed. In an emergency, always call 911.
