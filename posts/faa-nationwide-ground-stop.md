**Update, 6:40 p.m. PT: Ground stop lifted.** The FAA has lifted the nationwide ground stop. "At 8:15 tonight Eastern Time, we've opened up the airspace," Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy told reporters at the White House, which is 5:15 p.m. Pacific. The international NOTAM has also been cancelled, clearing flights to depart and to enter U.S. airspace.

Flights are departing again, but delays remain extreme. Duffy said the return to normal traffic will be slow, and airlines are warning that cancellations could stretch into the weekend. Thousands of U.S. flights were canceled Friday. If you're flying out of LAX, John Wayne, Ontario or Burbank tonight or this weekend, check your flight status with your airline before heading to the airport.

On the cause, Duffy pointed to instability with radar data, NBC News reported. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said new technology upgrades overloaded the existing system, and officials said there is no indication of a cyberattack.

**Update, 2:15 p.m. PT:** The ground stop remains in effect. Preliminary reports indicate it was not caused by a cyberattack, two law enforcement officials told CNN. An FAA advisory lists the stop through 10 p.m. Eastern (7 p.m. Pacific), though departures remain grounded until further notice. An internal Alaska Airlines message seen by CNBC described communications and radar problems. In its own statement, the FAA said the communication issues are affecting "multiple" Air Route Traffic Control Centers. FlightAware listed more than 7,800 delayed U.S. flights and about 380 cancellations.

The Federal Aviation Administration ordered a rare nationwide ground stop Friday afternoon, halting departing flights at airports across the United States. The order, issued by the FAA's Air Traffic Control System Command Center at 12:54 p.m. Pacific time, cites "extraordinary circumstances" and "reasons of safety" and directs all departing traffic to remain grounded until further notice. Inbound international flights are being managed case by case.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said in a post on X that the FAA issued the ground stop for all U.S. airports following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers, and that FAA technicians are investigating. He said the department would provide immediate updates.

A government source told CNN the communication issue began in the Northeast before spreading across the country.

An airline industry source told the OC Scanner News Desk the disruptions involve air traffic control radio frequencies and radar systems. The FAA has not publicly confirmed a radar outage, and the precise cause of the problem has not been confirmed.

## Southern California airports

The nationwide restriction includes major Southern California airports, including Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), John Wayne Airport (SNA), Ontario International Airport (ONT) and Hollywood Burbank Airport (BUR).

Travelers throughout Orange County and the greater Los Angeles region should prepare for potentially significant flight delays and disruptions as airlines await further instructions from federal aviation authorities. Check your airline's app or website for the status of your flight before heading to the airport.

## What we don't know yet

- The FAA has not provided a confirmed timeline for normal departures to resume. The advisory directs departing aircraft to remain grounded until further notice.
- It remains unclear how long the restrictions will remain in effect or whether additional measures will be necessary.

The OC Scanner News Desk is monitoring this developing situation, including updates from the FAA's Air Traffic Control System Command Center and impacts on Southern California airports.
