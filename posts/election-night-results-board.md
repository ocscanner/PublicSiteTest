Election night usually means jumping between a dozen tabs. Our **Elections** page puts it in one place: every House seat, Senate race and governor's race in the Nov. 3 midterms, California's 14 statewide propositions, and the local races on ballots in Orange and Los Angeles counties. It's live now at **[ocscanner.media/elections](https://ocscanner.media/elections)**, and results start filling in after polls close on Nov. 3.

## The national board

The top of the page tracks the balance of power in the House, the Senate and governors' mansions. Below it, tabs split the ballot: **House** (435 races), **Senate** (35), **Governors** (36), **CA Props**, **Orange County** and **LA County**.

Every race has its candidates loaded ahead of time, so you can look anything up now. Filter to **Close** races (within 5 points), **Flips**, **Called** or **Not called**, or search by name. **Latest calls** shows each race the moment it's called, and you can select any race to copy a link to it.

## Zoom into any state

![California's 52 House districts, zoomed in on the 48th](posts/img/elections/district-zoom.jpg)

Tap a state and the map zooms in. On the House tab you'll see its congressional districts, drawn on the new 2026 lines, each numbered and colored by who's won or leading. On the Senate and governor tabs, states that publish county-level counts are shown county by county, with stronger color for a wider lead. Tap a district or county to move in one level closer, which helps in crowded places like Los Angeles or the Bay Area.

## California propositions

![All 14 California propositions on one scoreboard](posts/img/elections/props.jpg)

All 14 statewide measures, Props 1–5 and 37–45, sit on one scoreboard with the Yes and No share for each. Select a proposition for its official title and for how Orange County voted on it. A proposition passes with a simple majority, but California counts mail ballots for weeks, so close ones can take days to call.

## Orange County and LA County

![The Orange County tab: 152 local races](posts/img/elections/orange-county.jpg)

The **Orange County** tab lists all 152 local races on the county's ballot: two Board of Supervisors seats, 77 city races in 31 cities, 47 school and college board races, 12 water and special districts, and Orange County's share of the vote in 14 state legislative races. Local measures appear once the Registrar of Voters posts its first count after 8 p.m.

![The LA County tab: the 12 races that went to November runoffs](posts/img/elections/la-county.jpg)

The **LA County** tab starts with the 12 races that went to runoffs in June, including Los Angeles mayor and county sheriff. Once the county posts its full Nov. 3 results, every local contest on its ballot fills in.

## Build your own watch list

![Two races saved to a personal watch list](posts/img/elections/watching.jpg)

Tap the star next to any race to watch it; the **Watching** filter then shows only those. Your list stays on your device, and **Chime on watched calls** plays a tone when one of them is called.

## Where the numbers come from

Vote counts come straight from state election offices and the Orange and LA County registrars, and refresh about every 10 minutes as they count. In states without a public results feed, the OC Scanner desk enters the numbers. All counts are unofficial until each state certifies.

**Race calls are made by OC Scanner**, and a seat only shows as won once it's been called. Until then, the map shows who's leading. We'd rather be right than first.
